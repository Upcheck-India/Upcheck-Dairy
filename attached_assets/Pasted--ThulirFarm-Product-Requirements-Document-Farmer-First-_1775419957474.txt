## 1. Executive Summary

ThulirFarm is a voice-first, offline-capable dairy operations mobile platform built with React Native, designed to help Indian dairy farmers increase milk yield, prevent losses, track income transparently, and get immediate help during emergencies. Unlike traditional agri-apps that overwhelm farmers with feature-heavy modules and dense text interfaces, ThulirFarm is structured around the actual daily workflows of an Indian dairy farmer — from the first morning milking at 5 AM to the evening accounting of income and expenses.

The application is specifically architected for the realities of rural India: intermittent connectivity, low-literacy users who prefer voice over text, Tamil-language-first interactions, and a mobile device that may be shared among family members. The entire UX philosophy is built around the principle that a farmer who opens the app every morning, uses it during problems, and tracks money daily has achieved true product-market fit.

This upgraded PRD (v2.0) incorporates a comprehensive assessment conducted from the perspective of an Indian dairy farmer, identifying critical gaps in the original specification and introducing enhancements across visual design, interaction patterns, language support, data intelligence, and native mobile capabilities. Every recommendation in this document has been evaluated against the question: "Will this make a real difference in a farmer's daily life, or is it just another feature?"

### 1.1 Core Promise

> **"Open ThulirFarm every day — it tells you what to do and helps you earn more."**

### 1.2 What Changed in v2.0

The following table summarizes the key upgrades made through the farmer-assessment lens, organized by impact area. Each change was evaluated against real farming scenarios in Tamil Nadu and across India.

| Area | Original (v1.0) | Upgraded (v2.0) |
|---|---|---|
| Design System | Generic UI, not farmer-specific | Green/earth tones, 56px touch targets, large Tamil typography, morning/evening context awareness |
| Language | English-only interface | Full Tamil bilingual UI with Noto Sans Tamil, voice I/O in ta-IN and en-IN |
| Navigation | Feature-first sections | 4-tab bottom navigation matching farmer mental model: Animals, Help, Money, Today |

## 2. Product Vision & Mission

### 2.1 Vision

To become the default daily assistant for every dairy farmer in India. ThulirFarm envisions a future where no farmer starts their day without opening the app, where every health issue is caught early through intelligent monitoring, and where every rupee earned and spent is transparently tracked. The app should feel less like a software tool and more like a trusted farmhand who knows every animal by name, remembers every transaction, and never forgets a task.

### 2.2 Mission

- **Help farmers make better daily decisions** by surfacing the right information at the right time, without requiring the farmer to search or navigate complex menus.
- **Reduce uncertainty in dairy operations** by providing AI-powered health diagnosis, milk anomaly detection, and financial reconciliation that catches problems humans might miss.
- **Increase profitability per animal** through data-driven insights into feeding efficiency, health interventions, and expense optimization.
- **Bridge the literacy gap** by making the entire app usable through voice commands in Tamil and English, with visual-first interfaces that minimize text dependency.
- **Empower women dairy operators** who manage the majority of India's small dairy operations, with accessible design and family-friendly account structures.

## 3. Farmer Assessment Framework

This is the most critical addition to the v2.0 PRD. Every feature, design decision, and technical choice has been evaluated through the lens of our primary persona: a Tamil Nadu dairy farmer. This section documents the assessment methodology and the key insights that drove the upgrades throughout this document.

### 3.1 Primary Persona: Kumar

| Attribute | Details |
|---|---|
| Name | Kumar (42 years old) |
| Location | Salem district, Tamil Nadu |
| Education | 8th standard, reads Tamil slowly |
| Farm Size | 4 cows (2 Jersey, 1 Murrah buffalo, 1 local) + 1 calf |
| Daily Routine | 5 AM: Morning milking, 6 AM: Feed & clean, 10 AM: Field work, 4 PM: Evening milking, 7 PM: Accounts |
| Phone | Redmi Note 12 (Android 13), 4GB RAM, 64GB storage |
| Connectivity | Jio 4G (frequent drops), 1.5GB/day data plan |
| Digital Literacy | Uses WhatsApp voice messages, watches YouTube videos, has never used an Excel sheet |
| Language | Thinks in Tamil, understands basic English numbers |
| Income | ₹12,000–18,000/month from milk sales |
| Pain Points | Doesn't know if cow is producing less milk, forgets vaccination dates, can't track if milk coop is paying correctly |
| Motivation | Wants his son to continue dairy farming; wants to earn more per litre |

*Table 2: Primary Persona — Kumar, Dairy Farmer from Tamil Nadu*

### 3.2 Key Assessment Insights

#### 3.2.1 Visual-First, Not Text-Heavy

Kumar does not read paragraphs. He recognizes icons, colors, and large numbers. The original PRD specified a standard feature list interface, but our assessment reveals that every screen must be scannable in under 3 seconds. Key numbers (today's milk, today's income) must be visible immediately without scrolling. Color coding is essential: green for healthy/good, yellow for attention needed, red for critical/action required. The UI must use a maximum of 20 words per screen, with Tamil translations for all labels.

#### 3.2.2 Single-Hand Operation

A dairy farmer holds a bucket in one hand and a phone in the other during morning milking. Every interactive element must have a minimum touch target of 56px (compared to the Material Design minimum of 48px). Bottom navigation is mandatory because the thumb naturally rests at the bottom of the phone. The most critical actions (milk logging, SOS help) must be reachable in a single tap from any screen.

#### 3.2.3 Morning/Evening Context

A farmer's day has two distinct halves centered around milking sessions. The original PRD treated the app as a static tool, but our assessment shows the app must be context-aware. When opened before 10 AM, the app should prioritize morning milk logging and today's task list. When opened after 3 PM, it should show evening milking, daily income summary, and tomorrow's preparations. This contextual adaptation significantly reduces cognitive load.

#### 3.2.4 Tamil Is Not Optional

The original PRD mentioned voice support but did not specify language. Our assessment makes Tamil language support a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have. This includes: full UI labels in Tamil alongside English, voice input in Tamil (ta-IN), voice output/advice in Tamil, number formatting in Indian convention (lakhs/crores), and date formatting in the Tamil calendar style. The farmer must be able to use the app entirely in Tamil without ever reading English text.

#### 3.2.5 Trust Through Transparency

Indian dairy farmers selling to cooperatives often suspect they are being underpaid due to fat/SNF miscalculation or quantity disputes. The Money section must go beyond simple tracking and provide an Expected vs. Reconciliation feature that alerts the farmer when received payment does not match expected payment based on logged quantities. This single feature can be the primary retention driver.

#### 3.2.6 Cow Identity, Not Just Names

Farmers recognize their cows by visual appearance, not database IDs. Each animal profile must support a photo (taken with the phone camera), and the app should display a large emoji or animal icon as a visual identity marker. Buffalo vs. cow distinction is culturally important in Tamil Nadu and must be visually represented (different emoji: 🐄 cow vs. 🐃 water buffalo).

#### 3.2.7 Celebration Drives Retention

When a farmer logs a higher-than-usual milk yield, the app should celebrate with a simple animation (confetti, green flash). When all daily tasks are completed, a congratulatory message should appear. These micro-rewards create positive emotional associations with the app, similar to the dopamine hits from social media likes, but tied to productive farm behavior.

## 4. Product Philosophy

### 4.1 Core Principle

> **Decision-first daily tool, NOT a feature-first super app.**

Every feature must pass the "Daily Use Test": Will the farmer use this feature at least once in a typical day? If not, it belongs in a secondary menu or a later roadmap phase. The app's success is measured by daily active usage, not by the number of features shipped.

### 4.2 Farmer Mental Model (UX Structure)

The entire app is structured into 4 core sections that map directly to a farmer's mental model of their daily work, accessible via a bottom tab bar that is always visible:

| Icon | Section | Tamil | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🐄 | My Animals | என் மாடுகள் | Monitor cows, log milk, health status |
| 🚨 | Problems & Help | உதவி | Instant emergency diagnosis and help |
| 💰 | Money | பணம் | Track earnings, expenses, detect losses |
| 📅 | Today | இன்று | Daily tasks, alerts, morning/evening plan |

*Table 3: App Navigation Structure — Farmer Mental Model*

### 4.3 Additional Navigation: Voice (Central Button)

A prominent floating microphone button sits at the center of the bottom tab bar, visually elevated above the other tabs. This is not just another navigation item but the primary interaction method for voice-first users. Tapping it opens a full-screen voice overlay where the farmer can speak any command in Tamil or English, and the app processes it using AI-powered NLP. This design decision ensures that even farmers who cannot read can use 100% of the app's functionality through voice alone.

## 5. Target Users

### 5.1 Primary Users

**Small dairy farmers with 2–10 animals:** This is the largest addressable segment in India, representing over 70% of dairy households. These farmers typically have low literacy levels, limited smartphone experience, and depend on dairy as their primary or secondary income source. The app must be optimized for their specific constraints: small screen text readability, voice-first input, and minimal data usage.

**Low-literacy, voice-first users:** The app must be fully functional without reading any text. Every action must have a voice equivalent, and all critical information must be communicated through audio playback in Tamil.

### 5.2 Secondary Users

**Women dairy operators:** In Tamil Nadu and across South India, women manage the majority of milking operations and daily animal care. The app must be designed with accessibility considerations including larger touch targets, high-contrast visuals, and support for shared family devices where multiple users may access the same app.

**Medium farmers with 10–20 animals:** These farmers manage slightly larger herds and may have one or two hired laborers. The app should support multi-user scenarios and provide herd-level analytics in addition to individual animal tracking.

### 5.3 Tertiary Users

**Commercial farms with 20+ animals:** These users will benefit from Phase 2 features like IoT sensor integration, bulk data import/export, and veterinary teleconsultation. The v2.0 MVP does not specifically target this segment but ensures the data architecture supports future scaling.

## 6. UX/UI Design System

This section defines the complete visual and interaction design system for ThulirFarm, built specifically for the Indian dairy farmer context. Every decision is grounded in the assessment insights from Section 3.

### 6.1 Color Palette

The color system uses natural, earthy tones that resonate with the farming context. Green represents growth, health, and the primary brand identity. Warm amber/gold represents money, income, and positivity. Earth brown provides grounding and warmth. Red is reserved exclusively for danger, losses, and emergencies.

| Role | Color | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Green `#16a34a` | Buttons, active states, positive indicators, brand identity |
| Secondary | Amber `#d97706` | Money section, income highlights, accent elements |
| Background | Warm Off-White `#fefce8` | App background, warm and inviting feeling |
| Card Background | White `#ffffff` | Cards, dialogs, input fields |
| Success/Healthy | Bright Green `#22c55e` | Health status: healthy, positive trends, task complete |
| Warning/Monitor | Orange `#f97316` | Health status: monitor, milk anomaly, attention needed |
| Danger/Critical | Red `#ef4444` | Health status: critical, emergency, loss detected |
| Income | Green `#22c55e` | Money received, income transactions, profit |
| Expense | Red `#ef4444` | Money spent, expense transactions, loss |
| Body Text | Dark Green `#1a2e05` | Primary text color, high contrast on light backgrounds |
| Muted Text | Olive `#4d7c0f` | Secondary text, descriptions, timestamps |

*Table 4: ThulirFarm Color Palette*

### 6.2 Typography

Typography is critical for the low-literacy user segment. All fonts must be significantly larger than standard app defaults, with particular attention to Tamil script legibility at small sizes.

| Element | Font | Size | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| App Title | System Bold | 24px | Bold |
| Section Header | Noto Sans Tamil Bold | 20px | Bold |
| Card Title | Noto Sans Tamil | 18px | Semi-Bold |
| Body Text (English) | Roboto Regular | 16px | Regular |
| Body Text (Tamil) | Noto Sans Tamil | 16px | Regular |
| Key Numbers | Roboto Bold | 36px+ | Bold |
| Button Text | Noto Sans Tamil | 18px | Bold |
| Caption/Timestamp | Roboto | 12px | Regular |

*Table 5: Typography System*

### 6.3 Touch Targets & Spacing

All interactive elements must meet a minimum touch target of **56px × 56px**, exceeding the Material Design 48px minimum. This accounts for rough farmer hands, calluses, and the imprecise thumb reach when holding a phone in one hand while working. Critical action buttons (SOS, milk log, voice) should be 72px or larger.

### 6.4 Component Design Patterns

#### 6.4.1 Animal Card

Each animal is represented as a card with the following structure: a large emoji icon (cow or buffalo) on the left, the animal's name in English and Tamil in the center with a colored health status badge, and today's total milk output as a large number on the right. Below this primary row, a 7-day mini bar chart shows the milk production trend at a glance, using green bars for normal days and highlighting today in a brighter green. Tapping the card expands it to reveal morning/evening breakdown, quick action buttons (Log Milk, Report Problem), and detailed health information.

#### 6.4.2 SOS Button (Problems & Help)

The centerpiece of the Help tab is a massive red circular button with pulsing animation, labeled with both Tamil and English text: **"ஏதாவது தவறா? / Something Wrong?"** The pulsing animation draws immediate attention and communicates urgency. Surrounding this button are quick-tap presets for common problems (fever, not eating, injury, in heat, milk drop), each displayed as large rounded buttons with emoji icons. These presets allow farmers to report common issues with a single tap without needing to use voice.

#### 6.4.3 Money Summary Cards

The Money tab opens with three summary cards displayed horizontally: This Month Income (green), This Month Expenses (red), and Profit (green if positive, red if negative). Each card displays a single large number with minimal labeling. Below these cards, a 7-day bar chart shows daily income vs. expenses using green and red bars respectively. The Expected vs. Received section uses a clear comparison layout with color-coded loss indicators.

#### 6.4.4 Task Cards

Each task is displayed as a card with a colored left border indicating priority: red pulsing for critical, red for high, orange for normal, and green for low. The task title is shown in both Tamil and English with a large checkbox for completion. Tapping a completed task triggers a subtle celebration animation (green checkmark with scale animation). Tasks are sorted by priority, with critical items always visible at the top.

## 7. Core Features & Specifications

### 7.1 My Animals (🐄 என் மாடுகள்)

The My Animals section provides real-time visibility into herd productivity and health. It is designed as the farmer's primary reference point, opened first thing in the morning to check on each animal before milking begins.

#### 7.1.1 Animal Registration

Farmers can register new animals through a bottom sheet dialog that supports both manual input and voice input. The registration form collects the animal's name (which becomes the primary identifier, not a system ID), breed (selected from a visual grid of common Indian breeds with Tamil labels), and optional age. Photo capture uses the device camera with an optional overlay guide for consistent framing. Each animal is automatically assigned a unique system ID internally, but the farmer never sees or needs to interact with it.

#### 7.1.2 Milk Logging

Milk logging is the single most important daily action and must be achievable in **under 5 seconds**. The primary flow is voice-based: the farmer taps the voice button and says "Lakshmi 5 litre morning" (or in Tamil). The AI voice parser extracts the animal name, quantity, and time of day, then displays a confirmation card before saving. A manual fallback allows selection from the animal grid, a large number pad for quantity entry, and a morning/evening toggle.

#### 7.1.3 Milk Anomaly Detection

The anomaly detection engine runs automatically each time milk is logged. It compares the current day's output per animal against a 3-day rolling average and triggers an alert when the drop exceeds 15%. Alerts appear as orange/red banners at the top of the My Animals view with clear messaging: *"Lakshmi's milk reduced by 20% — Check for health issues."* The system also updates the animal's health status to "Monitor" or "Critical" based on the severity of the drop (15–30% is Monitor, above 30% is Critical).

#### 7.1.4 Health Status Engine

Each animal has a continuously updated health status determined by multiple signals:

- Milk anomaly detection (weight: high)
- Reported symptoms through Problems & Help (weight: very high)
- Missed feeding logs (weight: medium)
- Approaching vaccination/health check due dates (weight: low)

The health status is displayed as a color-coded badge: 🟢 Healthy, 🟡 Monitor, 🔴 Critical.

#### 7.1.5 Quick Health Actions

Each animal card provides quick-action buttons for the most common farmer interventions: **Not Eating, Fever, Injury, and In Heat.** Tapping any of these pre-fills the Problems & Help diagnosis flow with the relevant animal and symptom, reducing the steps from problem identification to actionable advice.

---

### 7.2 Problems & Help (🚨 உதவி)

This is the most critical feature from a farmer's perspective. When a cow is sick, the farmer is stressed, time-sensitive, and needs immediate actionable advice. The design must prioritize speed and clarity above all else.

#### 7.2.1 SOS Button Flow

1. Farmer taps the large red SOS button at the center of the Help screen.
2. Voice recording begins automatically with a clear "Listening..." visual indicator.
3. Farmer speaks the problem in Tamil or English (e.g., *"Cow not eating since yesterday, looks tired"*).
4. Web Speech API transcribes the speech in real-time, displaying the text so the farmer can verify.
5. The transcribed text is sent to the AI diagnosis endpoint (LLM-powered backend).
6. Results are displayed in a structured Problem Result Card with bilingual output.
7. The Tamil advice section is automatically read aloud using Text-to-Speech.

#### 7.2.2 AI Diagnosis Response Structure

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `summary` | Brief problem summary in both Tamil and English, confirming what the system understood |
| `possibleCauses` | List of 2–4 most likely causes in simple language |
| `immediateActions` | Step-by-step actions the farmer can take right now (check temperature, give water, isolate) |
| `riskLevel` | Color-coded severity: low (green), medium (yellow), high (orange), critical (red) |
| `medicine` | Suggested over-the-counter medicine if applicable, with dosage in farmer-friendly terms |
| `tamilAdvice` | Detailed advice paragraph in spoken Tamil that a farmer can understand and act on |
| `homeRemedy` | Simple home remedy if applicable (turmeric paste, warm water massage, etc.) |
| `nextSteps` | What to do if the condition doesn't improve in 6–12 hours |

*Table 6: AI Diagnosis Response Structure*

#### 7.2.3 Escalation Logic

| Condition | System Action |
|---|---|
| Risk Level: Critical | Display prominent "Call Vet Now" button with phone number; send push notification to designated veterinarian contact |
| No improvement in 6 hours | Send reminder notification to farmer; escalate animal status to Critical |
| Repeat issue (same symptom within 7 days) | Suggest veterinary farm visit; log pattern for future AI model improvement |
| Risk Level: High | Display "Schedule Vet Visit" button; add task to Today's task list |
| Risk Level: Medium | Display home care instructions; set 12-hour follow-up reminder |
| Risk Level: Low | Display advice; log for monitoring; no urgent action required |

*Table 7: Escalation Logic Matrix*

---

### 7.3 Money (💰 பணம்)

The Money section addresses the farmer's need for financial clarity and trust. It goes beyond basic bookkeeping to provide reconciliation capabilities that catch payment discrepancies — a major pain point when selling to dairy cooperatives.

#### 7.3.1 Income Tracking

Milk-based income is the primary revenue stream. The system automatically calculates expected income based on logged milk quantities and a configurable rate per litre (default: ₹42/L, adjustable by the farmer). Additional income sources (calf sales, government subsidies) can be manually added. Income is displayed in Indian number format (e.g., ₹1,25,000 for 1.25 lakhs) with clear date grouping.

#### 7.3.2 Payment Reconciliation

This is a v2.0 critical addition. The Expected vs. Received section compares the farmer's logged milk quantities (converted to expected payment at the configured rate) against actual received payments. When a discrepancy is detected (e.g., expected ₹500 but received ₹450), the system highlights the loss in red with a clear message: *"Loss: ₹50 — Did the cooperative deduct for low fat/SNF?"* This feature empowers farmers to question unfair deductions and builds trust in the app as a financial guardian.

#### 7.3.3 Expense Tracking

Expenses are categorized into four primary buckets:

- 🌾 Feed (தீவனம்)
- 💊 Medicine (மருந்து)
- 👷 Labor (வேலை)
- 📦 Other (மற்றவை)

Each category uses a distinct icon and color for visual differentiation. Expense entries support voice input ("Feed expense 500 rupees") and manual entry with amount, description, and optional photo of receipt.

#### 7.3.4 Profit Calculation

Daily, weekly, and monthly profit is calculated as:

> **Profit = Total Income – Total Expenses**

The profit figure is prominently displayed with color coding: green for positive profit, red for loss. A trend line shows profit direction over the past 30 days.

---

### 7.4 Today (📅 இன்று)

The Today section is the farmer's daily dashboard and task engine. It drives daily engagement by providing a clear, actionable plan for the day.

#### 7.4.1 Context-Aware Greeting

The Today tab opens with a time-aware greeting:
- Before 12 PM: *"🌅 காலை வணக்கம், விவசாயி!" (Good morning, Farmer!)*
- After 3 PM: *"🌆 மாலை வணக்கம்" (Good evening)*

The greeting is followed by today's date in both Tamil calendar and English formats, a weather summary (mock in MVP, real API in Phase 2), and a time-of-day indicator icon.

#### 7.4.2 Today's Stats Bar

A horizontally scrollable stats bar displays four key metrics:

1. Total milk produced today (with morning/evening breakdown)
2. Number of animals healthy vs. needing attention
3. Total income today
4. Task completion ratio (e.g., 3/5 tasks completed)

#### 7.4.3 Task Sources and Generation

Tasks are generated from multiple sources and automatically prioritized:

- Animal lifecycle events (calving due dates, vaccination schedules)
- Health alerts triggered by anomaly detection
- Feeding schedules (mineral mix reminders, water trough cleaning)
- Breeding events (heat detection, AI scheduling)
- Manual farmer-created tasks

#### 7.4.4 Smart Reminders

The reminder system is context-aware and non-intrusive. Critical reminders appear immediately as push notifications. The system avoids notification fatigue by limiting non-critical reminders to a maximum of 3 per day and grouping related reminders.

## 8. Voice System

The voice system is ThulirFarm's most important differentiator and the primary interaction method for the target user segment. It must be reliable, fast, and work in both Tamil and English.

### 8.1 Phase 1: Command-Based Voice (MVP)

| Command (English) | Command (Tamil) | Action |
|---|---|---|
| "Lakshmi 5 litre morning" | "லக்ஷ்மி 5 இலிட்டர் காலை" | Log milk: Lakshmi, 5L, morning |
| "Rani 3 litres evening" | "ராணி 3 இலிட்டர் மாலை" | Log milk: Rani, 3L, evening |
| "Cow not eating" | "மாடு சாப்பிடுகவில்லை" | Report problem: not eating |
| "Lakshmi sick fever" | "லக்ஷ்மிக்கு காய்ச்சல்" | Report problem: fever for Lakshmi |
| "Feed expense 500" | "தீவனம் 500 ரூபாய்" | Add expense: feed, ₹500 |
| "Medicine 300 rupees" | "மருந்து 300 ரூபாய்" | Add expense: medicine, ₹300 |
| "Show my money" | "பணத்தை காட்டு" | Navigate to Money tab |
| "My animals" | "என் மாடுகள்" | Navigate to Animals tab |

*Table 8: Voice Command Reference (Phase 1)*

### 8.2 Voice Technical Implementation

The voice system is implemented using the **React Native Voice** library for speech-to-text input, supporting Tamil (ta-IN) and English (en-IN) language codes. For text-to-speech output, the app uses the **React Native TTS** library, configured with Tamil as the default language. Voice command parsing uses an LLM-powered backend endpoint that accepts raw transcribed text and returns structured JSON with the matched action and extracted parameters.

### 8.3 Phase 2: Conversational Assistant (Post-MVP)

Phase 2 envisions a fully conversational voice assistant where the farmer can have natural back-and-forth dialogue about their farm. Examples:

- *"How is Lakshmi doing this week?"* → Returns milk trend and health summary
- *"What should I do today?"* → Returns prioritized task list
- *"Am I making profit this month?"* → Returns financial summary with Tamil audio explanation

## 9. Data Model

The data model is designed for offline-first operation with local SQLite storage and background synchronization to a cloud PostgreSQL database. Each model includes timestamps for conflict resolution during sync operations.

### 9.1 Entity Relationship Overview

The system has five core entities: **Animal, MilkLog, Transaction, Task, and DailySummary.** Animals have a one-to-many relationship with MilkLogs and Tasks. MilkLogs record every milking session. Transactions record all financial movements. Tasks represent actionable items with priority and status tracking. DailySummary provides a daily aggregate snapshot for quick dashboard loading.

### 9.2 Animal Model

| Field | Type | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | String (CUID) | Auto | Unique identifier, system-generated |
| `name` | String | Yes | Primary identifier (farmer-facing) |
| `nameTamil` | String? | No | Tamil name for bilingual display |
| `breed` | String | Yes | Breed name (English) |
| `breedTamil` | String? | No | Breed name in Tamil |
| `age` | Int? | No | Age in years |
| `photo` | String? | No | Local file path to photo |
| `healthStatus` | String | Auto | `healthy` \| `monitor` \| `critical` |
| `tagColor` | String | Auto | `green` \| `yellow` \| `red` |
| `isActive` | Boolean | Auto | Soft delete flag, default `true` |
| `createdAt` | DateTime | Auto | Record creation timestamp |
| `updatedAt` | DateTime | Auto | Last modified timestamp |

*Table 9: Animal Model Schema*

### 9.3 MilkLog Model

| Field | Type | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | String (CUID) | Auto | Unique identifier |
| `animalId` | String | Yes | FK to Animal table |
| `quantity` | Float | Yes | Milk quantity in litres |
| `timeOfDay` | String | Yes | `morning` \| `evening` |
| `date` | DateTime | Auto | Timestamp of milking session |
| `notes` | String? | No | Optional notes about this session |
| `createdAt` | DateTime | Auto | Record creation timestamp |

*Table 10: MilkLog Model Schema*

### 9.4 Transaction Model

| Field | Type | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | String (CUID) | Auto | Unique identifier |
| `type` | String | Yes | `income` \| `expense` |

## 11. Offline-First Architecture

Offline capability is not an optional enhancement; it is a **fundamental architectural requirement**. Indian rural areas experience frequent connectivity drops, and farmers cannot afford to lose data when the network is unavailable.

### 11.1 Local Storage Strategy

The app uses **WatermelonDB** (built on SQLite) as the local database for reactive, offline-first data management. WatermelonDB provides schema-based records, reactive queries (UI updates automatically when data changes), lazy loading for performance, and built-in sync support. All data is stored locally first, then synced to the cloud database in the background. The sync engine uses a last-modified timestamp approach with conflict resolution based on a "last write wins" strategy.

### 11.2 Sync Architecture

1. All writes go to local SQLite first (zero-latency user experience)
2. A background sync service monitors connectivity changes using React Native NetInfo
3. When connectivity is detected, the sync service pushes pending local changes to the cloud API
4. After successful push, the service pulls any server-side changes
5. Conflict resolution uses `updatedAt` timestamps: the most recent write wins
6. Sync status is displayed as a subtle indicator (🟢 synced, 🟠 pending sync, 🔴 sync failed)
7. The user can manually trigger a sync by pulling down on any list view (pull-to-refresh)

### 11.3 Offline Features Matrix

| Feature | Offline Support | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Milk Logging | Full | Save locally, sync on reconnect |
| Animal Management | Full | CRUD operations work offline |
| Problem Diagnosis | Limited | Quick presets work offline; AI diagnosis requires network |
| Money Tracking | Full | All transactions saved locally |
| Task Management | Full | Create, complete, view tasks offline |
| Voice Commands | Full (STT) | Speech-to-text works offline; AI parsing requires network |
| Charts & Analytics | Cached | Shows last-synced data; refreshes on reconnect |
| Push Notifications | Queued | Delivered when device comes online |

## 13. User Flows

### 13.1 Milk Entry Flow (Voice)

1. Farmer opens ThulirFarm (auto-detects morning/evening context)
2. Farmer taps the central microphone button (1 tap)
3. Farmer speaks: *"Lakshmi 5 litre morning"*
4. App transcribes and displays confirmation: *"✔ Lakshmi — 5L — Morning"*
5. App auto-saves after 3 seconds unless farmer taps to cancel
6. Celebration animation if milk is higher than 3-day average

**Total: 2 taps + 1 voice command. Time: under 5 seconds.**

### 13.2 Milk Entry Flow (Manual)

1. Farmer opens Animals tab and taps "Log Milk" banner (1 tap)
2. Select animal from visual grid (1 tap)
3. Enter quantity using large number pad (1–2 taps)
4. Morning/Evening is auto-detected; farmer confirms (0–1 tap)
5. Tap "Save" button (1 tap)

**Total: 4–5 taps. Time: under 15 seconds.**

### 13.3 Emergency Problem Reporting

1. Farmer opens Help tab and taps the large red SOS button (1 tap)
2. Voice recording starts automatically; farmer speaks the problem
3. System transcribes, sends to AI, and displays structured diagnosis
4. Tamil advice is automatically read aloud via TTS
5. Farmer follows immediate actions or taps "Call Vet" if critical

**Total: 1 tap + 1 voice command. Time: under 30 seconds including AI processing.**

### 13.4 Daily Morning Routine

1. Morning notification at 5:30 AM: *"🐄 Time for morning milking!"*
2. Farmer opens app, sees Today tab with morning task list
3. Checks animal health summary (e.g., 3 healthy, 1 needs attention)
4. Logs milk for each animal using voice (4 voice commands, ~20 seconds)
5. Reviews anomaly alert if any animal's milk has dropped
6. Completes tasks (vaccination, mineral mix) by checking them off

**Total time: 3–5 minutes for the entire morning routine.**
